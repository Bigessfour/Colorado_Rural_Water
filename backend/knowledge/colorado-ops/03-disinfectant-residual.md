## Statewide residual expectation (Regulation 11 — as summarized by CDPHE Aqua Talk)

Colorado requires public drinking water systems to maintain continuous chemical disinfection (limited waiver / hand-pumped campground well exceptions exist under strict standards).

Regulation 11 requires a minimum disinfectant residual concentration of:

- **0.2 mg/L at the entry point** to the distribution system, and
- **0.2 mg/L in the distribution system**

These entry-point and distribution minima apply to groundwater (GW), GWUDI, and surface water (SW) systems. Source type still drives additional treatment (Groundwater Rule vs Surface Water Treatment Rule).

Most Colorado systems use free chlorine; chloramines are also used. Residual in the distribution system helps protect against contamination from backflow, leaks, low pressure, and similar pathways.

## Groundwater quick guide highlights (CDPHE Compliance Assurance, Nov 2010)

Online PDF: https://hermes.cde.state.co.us/islandora/object/co%3A21174/datastream/OBJ/download/Quick_guide__minimum_disinfection_for_groundwater.pdf
Also linked from: https://cdphe.colorado.gov/dbps and https://cdphe.colorado.gov/rtcr

### Overview

- All public water systems must chemically disinfect.
- Maintain a **detectable** residual disinfectant in the distribution system, measured at the **same time and place** as total coliform samples (including repeats).
- Maintain **at least 0.2 mg/L** residual at the **entry point** to the distribution system.
- Some systems must maintain a **system-specific** residual (may be greater than 0.2 mg/L) to provide at least **4-log** virus treatment when required.

### Distribution system

- Detectable = at or above the method detection limit (example: DPD colorimetric Standard Method 4500-Cl G cannot reliably measure below ~0.05 mg/L). Report non-detect when below the method detection limit.
- If more than **5%** of samples in a monitoring period lack a detectable residual for **two consecutive** monitoring periods → treatment technique violation → **Tier 2** public notification.
- Do not exceed **4.0 mg/L** running annual average (MRDL context for community / NTNC systems).
- Free chlorine systems: measure **free** chlorine as residual. Chloramine systems: report **total/combined** chlorine.

### Entry point (groundwater)

- Continuously chemically disinfect all sources whenever in use.
- Maintain ≥ **0.2 mg/L** at the entry point.
- Measure entry-point residual **at least weekly** when water is served from the groundwater source.
- If below 0.2 mg/L: sample at least every **24 hours** until restored.
- If not restored to ≥ 0.2 mg/L within **72 hours** → treatment technique violation → contact Water Quality Control Division ASAP (no later than next business day). Know: when it dropped, how long, lowest value. Rounding: 0.15 is not below 0.2; 0.14 is.
- Violation response includes WQCD notification, Tier 2 public notice, and documentation of failure / corrective / preventive steps.
- Sample after required contact time, before first customer when possible.

## Source-type extras (Aqua Talk)

- **GW:** Groundwater Rule — triggered source monitoring after TC+ **or** certify 4-log virus inactivation.
- **SW / GWUDI:** Surface Water Treatment Rule pathogen log removal/inactivation (Crypto 2-log, Giardia 3-log, viruses 4-log; Crypto may be higher per LT2). Filtration + disinfection are complementary barriers (see Drinking Water Policy 4 on CDPHE site).
